# Training opponent

`TrainingOpponent.Choose(MatchView, ActionOption[], BotContext?)` produces an `OpponentDecision` containing a command and a short explanation, or a null command when it must wait. The opponent only accepts the redacted view and legal actions for its seat. It never receives a `MatchEngine`, authoritative state, seed, replay, hidden hand, or deck order, so it cannot cheat. `BotContext` is its memory across one match: every face-up card it has seen in a public zone (it assumes three copies of a non-Unique card and one of a Unique), the turn it lobbied, how often it used each ability this turn, and what the engine refused (see below). Keep one per seat per match.

Since issue #642 it can answer ANY action the engine offers, and no card play scores below passing:

- **Completer** (`TrainingOpponent.Complete.cs`): fills in targets, multi-target slots, a player, a Gold payment, a cost option and a destination from the metadata the engine ships with each action.
- **Intent** (`AbilityIntent.cs`): a card-agnostic reading of an ability from its printed label and target category (destroy > bow > penalize > help; negate, draw, take or spend the Imperial Favor, gain or lose Honor). Nothing names a card, so a card wired tomorrow is scored the moment it exists. Hand-tuned scores for abilities it already understood stay as overrides.
- **Policy** (`TrainingOpponent.Policy.cs`, `BotProfile.cs`): Proclaim and the gain-versus-opponent-loses Honor choices follow the deck's Honor profile; draw abilities rise as the hand falls short of its maximum; Lobby and Favor-as-a-cost follow Family Honor and Personality counts; a Reaction that negates is always played and any other Reaction is a deterministic weighted choice.
- **Attacks** (`AttackPlanner.cs`): the army is split into as many sub-armies as each beat their province plus every unbowed unit that could defend it.
- **Battle policy** (`BattlePolicy.cs`, `TrainingOpponent.Battle.cs`, issue #788; see `docs/decisions/2026-10-09-bot-battle-policy.md`): the bot passes once a battle is decided (with the Honor, safe-move and threat-removal exceptions), aims Attacks at the highest Force they can destroy, bows by attachments or printed threat, sends home only what it can spare as a losing defender, claims first-action reactions only when it will use them, and uses the Border Keep refresh with 22+ Gold.
- **Refusals**: the engine lists a few actions it then rejects (`Soak` calls these gaps). Report a refusal with `BotContext.NoteRejection(version, command, error)` and call `Choose` again at the same version: it skips that answer, keeps the other targets of the same action and, for an attack, probes one unit at one province at a time. The practice client does this (`MatchSession.StepPracticeOpponent`).

It is still a modest practice opponent, not a competitive L5R player or a search-based AI. It uses deterministic tie breaks, and its one weighted choice is rolled from the match state so a replay makes the same choice.

Always execute its returned command through the authoritative engine. The UI should make practice mode explicit and invoke it only for the configured AI seat. Apply one decision at a time after the previous result is displayed, respecting reaction priority and pending choices. Never make two AI decisions against the same state version except to retry after a refusal.

The dependency-free tests run several seeded matches through completion, reject any illegal command, cap total decisions, replay completed results, and exercise assignment construction from the offered legal unit/province metadata. `tests/L5R.AI.Tests/BotPolicyTests.cs` pins each policy above, and the soak suite (`docs/BOT_SOAK.md`) plays random-deck bot-versus-bot matches over every wired card to prove it never stalls, loops or is stuck.
